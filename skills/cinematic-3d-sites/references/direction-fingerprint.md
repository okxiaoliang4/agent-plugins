# Direction fingerprint

A fingerprint is a set of linked artistic decisions. It prevents a reusable workflow from becoming a reusable look.

## Build the fingerprint

Choose one answer for each axis from the subject and audience, not from a preset library.

1. **Spatial metaphor** — What world makes this subject legible: observatory, archive, operating theatre, assembly line, excavation, stage, field instrument, or a metaphor specific to the domain?
2. **Composition** — Where does tension live: centered monument, asymmetric negative space, split specimen, deep tunnel, moving crop, layered diorama, or another deliberate frame?
3. **Camera grammar** — How does the viewer learn: macro inspection, orbital reveal, dolly-through, sectional cut, locked tableau, handheld drift, or a restrained combination?
4. **Material logic** — What physical behavior belongs to the subject: hydrated, fibrous, ceramic, machined, translucent, powder-coated, emissive, matte, or data-derived?
5. **Light logic** — What reveals form: microscope illumination, grazing studio light, projector scan, volumetric daylight, bioluminescence, instrument LEDs, or another motivated source?
6. **Interface voice** — What role does typography play: editorial narration, instrument readout, field notes, command console, museum label, cinematic title card, or an original system?
7. **Signature transition** — What transformation belongs uniquely to this subject: unfold, dock, slice, assemble, denoise, annotate, compare, crystallize, dissolve, or another causal gesture?

Bind the answers into one sentence: `A [spatial metaphor] where [camera grammar] reveals [subject] through [material/light logic], while [interface voice] records [signature transition].`

## Force divergence

Generate three fingerprints that disagree on at least four axes. Reject siblings that differ mainly by palette, typography, or camera speed. Select using the brief's communication goal, not personal taste.

Use references by decomposition: extract a reference's framing, rhythm, density, material response, or typography relationship. Recombine attributes from different sources instead of reproducing one source's surface.

## Template test

A direction is ready only when all are true:

- Removing color leaves a distinctive composition and camera grammar.
- Replacing the subject would break the signature transition.
- At least one quiet beat gives the eye somewhere to rest.
- Materials explain the subject rather than announcing "futuristic."
- The interface could not be pasted unchanged onto an unrelated 3D launch page.
- The hero frame and the most detailed frame feel like the same film.

If the test fails, change the spatial metaphor or camera grammar before polishing colors.
